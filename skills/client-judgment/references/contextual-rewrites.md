# Contextual rewrites

Diagnose the implication, not just the phrase. Never replace uncertainty with unsupported confidence. The examples below require the facts stated in their conditions.

| Wording to examine | Context and risk | Better approach |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Should be fine” | Recipient needs an evidenced readiness decision | If checks passed, state the scoped result. If not, state the decision-changing unknown. |
| “Let me know if anything breaks” | Used instead of establishing readiness | State relevant verification first. Retain a support offer only if appropriate to the agreement. |
| “Your old browser caused it” | Diagnosis unconfirmed or blame unnecessary | Describe the observed compatibility issue neutrally and the confirmed fix, if any. |
| “I'll try for Tuesday” | Actual status is an estimate | State Tuesday as an estimate with its relevant dependency. Do not upgrade it to a promise. |
| “As per my last email” | May sound accusatory in a strained exchange | Restate the necessary fact briefly, without reprimanding the recipient. |
| “No worries!” | Reply to confirmed business disruption | Acknowledge the concrete effect and supported recovery. The phrase can be fine for a routine favor. |
| “Just a quick addition” | Extra functionality affects scope | Explain the verified trade-off; offer assessed options. Do not invent their cost or delivery date. |

## Impact, recovery, prevention

For bad news, lead with the effect on the recipient's plan. Acknowledge the sender's confirmed responsibility once. State the recovery and any prevention work separately: a fix does not prove recurrence is impossible. Flag a known threat to a dependent campaign; do not call the campaign safe merely because a provider estimated two business days.

Do not promise to contact the recipient's customers or colleagues without authorization. A corrected attachment can be offered for onward sharing without assuming permission to distribute it yourself.

## Calm boundaries

Keep the relationship warm and the boundary explicit. Explain supported time/cost trade-offs without apologizing for the existence of costs. An alternative is useful when feasible; do not invent one to avoid saying no. Once a decision is agreed, confirm it concisely and proceed within authorization. Reopen it when material new facts change the risk.

## Ownership and coordination

A request to keep a campaign provisional may be appropriate when the client owns the send decision. Pair it with a supported sender follow-up when one exists; do not invent a deadline to make the message look accountable. A bounded recommendation or clearly stated next step is acceptable when no follow-up commitment has been established.
