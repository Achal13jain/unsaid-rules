# Audience journey checks

Use this checklist selectively for the recipient's intended use. A walkthrough of every feature is not required for every message. Use available evidence and authorized read-only checks; report unverified items accurately. This checklist does not authorize changes or submission of real inquiries, purchases, or other external transactions.

## Website or product

- Follow the relevant audience journey: landing page, navigation, key content, primary call to action, confirmation, and expected receipt. A successful-looking form screen is not proof that the inquiry arrived.
- Check visible links, icons, and buttons on that journey for real destinations. Distinguish deliberately disabled prototype controls from broken live interactions.
- Check for unintended placeholders, sample company names, test contact details, and exposed internal notes. A source TODO can remain after a feature is implemented; inspect rendered behavior before describing a customer-visible defect.
- Check company/contact details and visible privacy or terms pages for obvious missing content. Do not claim this establishes legal compliance.
- Use the agreed device/browser coverage and actual audience requirements. State only checks performed. Do not extrapolate one reported success to all versions.
- Confirm that observations concern the relevant deployment and revision. A staging pass can justify a staging preview, not a live-site claim.

## Documents and presentations

Check the relevant names, figures, dates, audience-facing pages, comments, revision marks, and internal notes. Distinguish a clearly labelled working draft from a final external deliverable. Do not declare an entire report verified after checking only its cover.

## Decide proportionately

Assess each gap by its consequence for the stated use. Blockers prevent that use; understood limitations may permit a bounded approval; unrelated or cosmetic issues need not delay a controlled preview. Do not turn every item into a launch gate or require perfect software.

If a gap matters, state the consequence, the supported next action, the known owner or required input, and timing only when established. If it does not matter to this decision, omit it from the short client reply. Never hide a consequential issue merely because fixing it is awkward.
