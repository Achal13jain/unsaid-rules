# Scenario examples

All examples are fictional. Each reply is constrained to the facts immediately above it. Adapt the reasoning, not the names or wording.

## 1. Independent prospect sharing: known blockers

**Facts:** Client asks if mobile/tablet is complete and whether prospects can browse the live site. Only two phones have been checked; no tablet result is available. The live inquiry form drops submissions. Legal pages display placeholder company details. No repair schedule or assigned follow-up is established.

**Reply:** “The site needs the inquiry form and company details resolved before prospects browse it: inquiries are not arriving, and the legal pages still contain placeholders. Mobile checks passed on two phones; tablet testing remains unconfirmed. Please send the approved company details for the legal pages.”

Do not add “today,” claimed fixes, or additional device coverage. Do not conceal the known blockers behind a vague final-review promise.

## 2. Verified readiness: direct approval

**Facts:** LinkedIn updated; agreed mobile/tablet checks and inquiry delivery verified on the live site; no known blockers. Client asks about interviews and starting German. German is in agreed scope, approved copy is ready, and sender is authorized to start next.

**Reply:** “The LinkedIn link is updated, and the mobile and tablet work is complete. Yes, the site is ready to share for your interviews. We can start the German version next using the approved copy.”

Do not ask for the copy again or invent an additional review.

## 3. Delay: no agreed update time

**Facts:** Friday delivery will be missed. Sender omitted a required provider review from the estimate. Provider estimates two business days; Tuesday is a target, not a guarantee. Client's Wednesday campaign depends on checkout. No check-in time is agreed. Sender's routine role includes provider follow-up and they have said they will chase the provider and update the client when they hear back; no time is set.

**Reply:** “Checkout won't be live Friday; I missed the provider review in my estimate. Tuesday is the current target, subject to approval, so Wednesday's campaign is at risk. I'll chase the provider and let you know as soon as I hear, so you can decide on the campaign timing with that in hand.”

The campaign decision stays with the client; the chase stays with the sender, tied to a trigger because no time is agreed. Outside the draft, recommend agreeing a check-in before the client's campaign decision deadline. Do not silently put Monday 10:00 into the draft, and do not send the earlier form of this reply (“the next step is to check with the provider; I'd keep the campaign provisional”): it names no one who will act.

## 4. Delay: a supported next update

**Facts:** Same delay as case 3. In addition, sender explicitly says they will check the review status and update the client Monday at 10:00, even if approval is still pending.

**Reply:** “Checkout won't be live Friday; I missed the provider review in my estimate. Tuesday is the current target, subject to approval. That puts Wednesday's campaign at risk. I'll check the status and update you Monday at 10:00, even if the review is still pending, so you can decide on the campaign timing.”

The update commitment does not guarantee the provider's answer or a successful launch.

## 5. Staging passed; deployment pending

**Facts:** Client asks if the live site is ready for a demo. Relevant checks passed on staging; production deployment remains pending. Sender says they will deploy now, verify the relevant demo checks in production, and message the client with the result; they ask only for reply wording. No actual deployment has occurred and no confirmation time is agreed.

**Reply:** “The staging checks have passed, but the update isn't live yet. I'll deploy now and message you as soon as it's confirmed on the live site; treat that message as the go-ahead for the demo.”

Do not deploy as part of drafting; the draft records the sender's stated intention, the sender performs it. Had the sender only said they *could* deploy, the draft would offer it (“I can deploy now and confirm once it's live”) rather than announce it. Do not imply a ten-minute deployment estimate guarantees live readiness in ten minutes. If deployment is separately authorized, act under the host's normal permissions and update the facts after checking the result.

## 6. Source warning versus live evidence

**Facts:** A pricing-source TODO exists. The user has verified that the live page displays the approved CMS price and that its contact link works.

**Reply:** “Yes, the pricing page is ready. The approved price is displayed correctly on the live site, and the contact link has been checked.”

With source evidence alone, flag the potential problem without claiming it is visible in production. A request to say all done does not justify an unsupported completion claim.

## 7. Additional work and commercial authority

**Facts:** A booking portal is explicitly outside the agreed brochure-site scope. No estimate exists. Sender may gather requirements; the PM approves pricing. Client asks for Friday delivery at no extra cost.

**Reply:** “The booking portal would extend the agreed scope. I can gather the requirements for the PM to assess timing and pricing, but I can't confirm Friday delivery or include it at the current price.”

If the contract scope is unknown, do not assert that an addition is out of scope merely because it takes time. Explain the effort and propose checking the agreement.

## 8. Routine confirmation

**Facts:** A long-term client asks “done?” about a corrected typo. The fix is deployed and checked live; the relationship is casual.

**Reply:** “Yep, fixed and checked on the live site.”

No audit, new offer, or disclaimer is needed.

## Other situations

- **Compatibility:** Acknowledge the reported symptom, ask only for missing useful diagnostics, and separate observed failure from suspected cause. Do not expand device coverage beyond evidence.
- **Dependencies:** Ask for specific approved text, decisions, or secure access. Explain which work depends on it and what is unaffected, only when known. Never ask for a password in ordinary chat.
- **Handover:** Separate delivery from formal acceptance. State the actual support term; do not imply perpetual maintenance.
- **Incident:** Distinguish mitigation, service restoration, cause, and prevention. An update time is not a resolution promise. Do not invent fault or absence of data loss.
