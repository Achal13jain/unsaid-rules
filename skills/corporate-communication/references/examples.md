## 1. A delay caused by missing instructions

**Audience and tone:** Manager, diplomatic.

**Wrote:** "I did the work, but nobody told me what to do next. Now they're blaming me for the delay."

**Send:** "I completed the assigned work, but I didn't receive the next steps, and that contributed to the delay. I'd like to make sure that context is understood, and to agree on how hand-offs should work so it doesn't happen again."

**Judgment:** The frustration becomes a clear account and a request to fix the process. The concern about blame stays, as a wish for the context to be understood, without accusing anyone or repeating the guess about what others think.

**Jargon version:** "Per my previous communications, the deliverables were actioned; however, downstream alignment was not socialized, which impacted timelines. Let's circle back to synergize on process going forward." It sounds formal, but it hides who did what and never says what the writer needs.

## 2. An overdue follow-up

**Audience and tone:** Colleague, warm and collaborative.

**Wrote:** "Sorry to bother you again, but I was just wondering whether you had a chance to look at the budget sheet I sent last week? No worries if not!"

**Send:** "Following up on the budget sheet I sent last week: could you take a look when you have a moment?"

**Judgment:** The apology and the self-minimizing go, and the politeness stays. "No worries if not" invited the reader to ignore the request, so the ask now stands on its own, while "when you have a moment" keeps the low pressure. No deadline or reason is added.

**Note to the user:** If you need it by a particular date, add the date and the reason.

## 3. More work than capacity

**Audience and tone:** Manager, firm but respectful.

**Wrote:** "I cannot keep accepting new tasks when I already have too much work."

**Send:** "I can't take on new tasks on top of my current workload. If this one is a priority, could you help me decide what to set aside to make room for it?"

**Judgment:** The refusal stays, and the writer's goal of protecting their capacity becomes a request for the manager to set priorities. Nothing promises to absorb the work, and the message does not name what to drop, because the writer did not.

## 4. Disagreeing with a manager's approach

**Audience and tone:** Manager, diplomatic.

**Wrote:** "honestly I think moving everything to the new tool mid-quarter is a bad idea, half the team hasn't been trained and we'll lose a week"

**Send:** "I have a concern about moving everything to the new tool mid-quarter. Half the team hasn't been trained on it yet, and I think we'd lose about a week. Could we talk about the timing before we commit?"

**Judgment:** "A bad idea" becomes a concern with its reasons, and the disagreement stays plain. The message does not pretend to support the plan, and the lost week remains the writer's estimate rather than a fact. Asking to discuss the timing expresses the writer's goal of reconsidering it.

## 5. An unrealistic deadline

**Audience and tone:** Manager, assertive.

**Wrote:** "there's no way this is done by friday, that's insane, we haven't even got the designs"

**Send:** "Friday isn't realistic while we're still waiting for the designs."

**Judgment:** The refusal and the reason stay; "that's insane" goes.

**Note to the user:** If you can estimate a date once the designs arrive, say so. The rewrite does not offer one for you.

## 6. A client asking for work beyond scope

**Audience and tone:** Client, client-friendly.

**Wrote:** "no we can't just add that, it's way more work than you think and it wasn't in the deal"

**Send:** "That feature isn't part of the agreed scope, and it's a larger piece of work than it may appear, so we can't add it as things stand."

**Judgment:** The no, the size of the work and the agreed scope all stay. "Than you think" goes, because it sounds dismissive.

**Note to the user:** If you can offer an estimate, add it yourself. The rewrite never offers work the writer did not offer.

**Jargon version:** "Unfortunately, this request falls outside the agreed parameters of the current engagement and would require significant additional bandwidth to action." It is wordy and cold, and it buries the plain answer.

## 7. A concern about a colleague, without accusing

**Audience and tone:** The colleague, diplomatic.

**Wrote:** "Raj keeps pushing untested code to main and it breaks everything for the rest of us"

**Send:** "Hi Raj, recent pushes to main have broken things for the rest of the team. Could we make sure changes are tested before they're merged?"

**Judgment:** The message describes the effect rather than judging Raj, and it asks for the change the writer wants. Whether the code was untested is the writer's inference, so the message asks for testing instead of asserting that it did not happen.

**Note to the user:** If you know the changes were untested, you can say so plainly. If you would rather raise this with your lead, say so and the message can be addressed to them.

## 8. An apology when responsibility is known

**Audience and tone:** Client, client-friendly.

**Wrote:** "my bad, I sent you the wrong invoice, the right one's attached"

**Send:** "Apologies, I sent you the wrong invoice earlier. The correct one is attached."

**Judgment:** The writer owns the mistake once and states the fix. There is no promise that it will never happen again, and no extra apology.

## 9. A problem whose cause is not yet known

**Audience and tone:** Manager, neutral professional.

**Wrote:** "the report numbers are wrong, sorry, maybe my formula messed it up or maybe the data export was bad, not sure"

**Send:** "Sorry for the trouble: the figures in the report are wrong. I'm not sure of the cause yet; it could be my formula or the data export."

**Judgment:** The apology covers the impact without admitting a cause, both possibilities stay, and the uncertainty stays. Nothing promises an investigation the writer did not mention.

**Note to the user:** If you are checking, say so, and say when you expect to know.

## 10. Asking for a raise

**Audience and tone:** Manager, persuasive and negotiation-oriented.

**Wrote:** "I've been doing the team lead job for 6 months since Priya left and I'm still paid as a junior, I want that fixed"

**Send:** "Since Priya left six months ago, I've been doing the team lead role while still being paid at the junior level. I'd like to discuss bringing my pay in line with the role. Could we find a time to talk about it?"

**Judgment:** The case rests on the facts the writer gave, and "I want that fixed" becomes a specific ask and a request to talk. It adds no achievements and does not ask for a new title, because the writer asked about pay.

**Note to the user:** One or two specific results from the past six months would strengthen the case. Add them if you have them.

## 11. An executive status update with a blocker

**Audience and tone:** Executive, executive and concise.

**Wrote:** "ugh the release slipped again bc the vendor api keeps breaking, we're stuck till they fix it, idk when"

**Send:** "The release has slipped again. The vendor's API keeps failing, and we're blocked until they fix it. We don't have a new date yet."

**Judgment:** The slip, the cause, the block and the missing date all stay, in three short sentences with the outcome first. "Ugh" and "idk" go, and the uncertainty stays honest.

**Note to the user:** If you know when you'll next update them, say so. The rewrite does not promise an update for you.

**Jargon version:** "Due to ongoing third-party integration challenges, the release timeline has been impacted. We are actively monitoring the situation and will provide updates as they become available." It is vague about the cause, and it promises monitoring and updates the writer never mentioned.

## 12. A message to a recruiter

**Audience and tone:** Recruiter, warm and collaborative.

**Wrote:** "hey saw you're hiring for data roles, I'm a data analyst with 4 yrs exp in SQL and python, open to chat?"

**Send:** "Hi, I saw that you're hiring for data roles. I'm a data analyst with four years of experience in SQL and Python, and I'd be glad to talk if you think there's a fit."

**Judgment:** It stays short and specific. It adds no claims the writer did not make, such as being an expert or passionate.

## 13. Asking for a decision

**Audience and tone:** Manager, executive and concise.

**Wrote:** "so do we go with vendor A or B? I need to know by thursday or we lose the discount, I've sent the comparison twice"

**Send:** "Could you decide between vendor A and vendor B by Thursday? After that, we lose the discount. The comparison is in my two earlier emails."

**Judgment:** The decision comes first, with the writer's own deadline and its reason. "I've sent the comparison twice" becomes a pointer to where it is, without the reproach.

## 14. A message that is already good

**Audience and tone:** Colleague, warm and collaborative.

**Wrote:** "Thanks for the quick turnaround on the slides. I've added two comments on page 4; everything else looks good to me."

**Send:** The message as written.

**Judgment:** It is clear, specific and warm. The right response is one line saying it already works, and the message unchanged.

## 15. A boundary with a colleague

**Audience and tone:** Colleague, assertive.

**Wrote:** "stop dumping your tickets on me at 6pm, I have my own stuff"

**Send:** "Please don't assign me extra tickets at 6 p.m.; I have my own work to do."

**Judgment:** The boundary and its reason stay. "Dumping" goes, because it turns a request into an accusation.

**Jargon version:** "I would appreciate it if we could align on ticket allocation best practices to optimize bandwidth across the team." It is so soft that the request disappears.

## 16. A quick status in chat

**Audience and tone:** Team channel, neutral professional.

**Wrote:** "fixed the login thing, still waiting on design for dashboard, nothing else blocked"

**Send:** "Login issue is fixed. The dashboard is waiting on design. Nothing else is blocked."

**Judgment:** All three facts stay, at the same length. A short message stays short.

**Jargon version:** "Quick update: the authentication workstream has been successfully remediated. The dashboard initiative remains pending design deliverables. No further blockers have been identified at this time." It turns three plain facts into three inflated ones.

## 17. Rough thoughts into meeting notes

**Audience and tone:** Team, neutral professional.

**Wrote:** "meeting w/ sara + dev team. agreed: launch moves to 12 june. sara owns the copy. still unsure about pricing page, nobody decided"

**Send:** "Meeting with Sara and the development team. Decisions: the launch moves to 12 June, and Sara owns the copy. Open: the pricing page, which has not been decided."

**Judgment:** Every decision, the owner, the date and the open item stay. The notes gain structure, and nothing is added.

## 18. Talking points for a difficult conversation

**Audience and tone:** Manager, spoken, empathetic and firm.

**Wrote:** "I need to tell my manager I'm burning out and can't keep working weekends"

**Send:**
- "There's something about my workload I'd like to talk about."
- "I've been working weekends, and I'm feeling burned out."
- "I can't keep working weekends."
- "Could we look at my priorities together so the work fits into the week?"

**Judgment:** Short spoken points, not a script. The writer wants to say they are burning out, so it stays, and so does the boundary. The closing ask expresses the writer's goal without promising anything about output.

## 19. A concern raised with HR

**Audience and tone:** HR, formal.

**Wrote:** "my manager is harassing me, he yells at me in every meeting, I want HR to do something"

**Send:** "I'd like to raise a concern about my manager's behavior toward me. He yells at me in every meeting we have. I consider this harassment, and I'd like HR's help in addressing it."

**Judgment:** It describes what happens in the writer's terms and keeps the writer's request. "Harassment" stays as the writer's own characterization, not as a legal conclusion, and nothing is softened or exaggerated.

**Note to the user:** HR may have a formal complaint process; it is worth asking about it. This is not legal advice, and a specialist can help if you need one.
